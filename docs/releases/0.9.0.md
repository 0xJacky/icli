- `log syslog` no longer misses the lines you filtered for on a busy device. It took only a process name and a level, so `--max-lines` filled up with every other subsystem's lines before a tweak's marker arrived, and the read came back `truncated` without it. New `--subsystem` (with its dotted children, so `com.example` keeps `com.example.net`), `--category`, `--message-contains` (case-sensitive) and `--pid` filters, and the minimum levels `debug`, `info` and `default`, all apply before the cap; `truncated` now means more matching lines arrived than were kept. `captureSyslog` takes the same filters, as parameters after its old ones or as a `SyslogFilter`.
- IcliKit can listen to the live unified log continuously. `SyslogStream` is one listen, so there is no seam between reads: it delivers each matching entry to a handler, in arrival order, until it is cancelled, its duration or entry limit is reached, the handler answers false or the system ends the stream, and says which. No entry arrives once `cancel()` returns, and releasing the stream cancels it. `syslogEntries` offers the same as an async sequence with a bounded buffer. A handler that falls behind makes the system drop entries, and `syslogEntries` drops entries beyond its buffer, rather than either growing without bound. Before, a host that wanted a long log had to stitch overlapping reads together and remove the duplicates.
- `log crash --max-bytes` and `readCrashLog(_:maxBytes:)` read at most that many bytes of a crash report, cut back to a whole character, instead of loading the whole file; the result reports `truncated`, the bytes returned and the whole file's `size`. A path that is not a regular file, such as a FIFO, is refused instead of blocking the read.
- When `app frontmost` cannot verify the frontmost app, its answer now includes a `runningboard` object saying why: RunningBoard unavailable, no process list, no process handles (with RunningBoard's error), no process states, no focal app, or several focal apps (named). A host whose frontmost answer differs from the CLI's can see which step failed. The verified answer is unchanged.
- A log line that is not valid Unicode (a lone UTF-16 surrogate in its message, process, subsystem or category) is reported with U+FFFD in place of the broken character, by `log syslog` and the stream alike, and a message with a NUL in it is kept whole.
- A host-side test of the log filters, the stream's life cycle and the bounded crash read runs in the release workflow.
